# ISC2 Certifications (2026 Guide)

This guide reflects the widely referenced **ISC2 portfolio as of September 2026**; ISC2 adjusts experience rules and CPE policy periodically, so verify on the official site before planning. Where CompTIA certifies fundamentals and Cisco certifies equipment expertise, **ISC2 certifies the governance-and-management tier of security** — its flagship CISSP is the certificate most often written into job ads for security manager, architect and CISO-track roles, and in many enterprises it is the difference between "senior engineer" and "head of" titles.

## The portfolio at a glance

| Certificate                                                     | Tier         | Experience required                       | Serves                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CC** — Certified in Cybersecurity                             | Entry        | None                                      | First security certificate; free training+exam via ISC2's "One Million Certified" program |
| **SSCP** — Systems Security Certified Practitioner              | Practitioner | 1 year in 1+ domain                       | Hands-on administrators/ops who implement controls                                        |
| **CISSP** — Certified Information Systems Security Professional | Leadership   | 5 years in 2+ domains (4 with waiver)     | Security managers, architects, consultants, CISO track                                    |
| **CCSP** — Certified Cloud Security Professional                | Specialist   | 5 years IT, 1 year cloud (CISSP waives 1) | Cloud security architects/engineers                                                       |
| **CGRC** — Governance, Risk and Compliance                      | Specialist   | 2 years in the domain                     | GRC analysts, authorization/assessment roles                                              |
| **CSSLP** — Secure Software Lifecycle                           | Specialist   | 4 years in SDLC                           | AppSec leads, secure-development champions                                                |

## CISSP: the one that changes job titles

CISSP tests **eight domains** at management depth — Security & Risk Management, Asset Security, Security Architecture & Engineering, Communication & Network Security, Identity & Access Management, Security Assessment & Testing, Security Operations, Software Development Security. The exam's famous difficulty is not technical trivia; it is answering as a **risk-minded manager**: the correct answer is rarely the most technical one, it is the one that best balances risk, business impact and cost.

Two gates besides the exam:

1. **Experience** — five years of paid, full-time work across at least two of the eight domains. A four-year degree (or approved cert) waives one year. If you pass with less experience, you become an **Associate of ISC2** with six years to earn the missing time — the exam result is banked.
2. **Endorsement** — an existing ISC2 member vouches for your experience and ethics; then you pay the Annual Maintenance Fee (AMF) and maintain **120 CPE credits over each 3-year cycle** (SSCP/CCSP-style certs: 60 per cycle).

**Prep reality:** 3–6 months for experienced engineers, built on one thick book (the official ISC2 guides), a question bank with 2,500+ practice questions to internalize the "think like a manager" style, and coverage of the newer exam additions (AI security, cloud, zero trust, DPO topics). A common, effective order for a generalist: CompTIA Security+ → CISSP once real experience exists — chasing CISSP too early mostly teaches the exam, not the job (see [CompTIA Certifications](/certifications/comptia-certifications)).

## CC: the free door in

ISC2's "One Million Certified in Cybersecurity" initiative made the entry-level **CC** free (self-paced training + exam). It is the lightest legitimate first certificate for career-switchers: security principles, basic cryptography, network security, incident response basics. It carries the same AMF/CPE obligations after the first year — worth knowing before collecting certificates nobody requires.

## SSCP vs CISSP: implementer vs decision-maker

The pair confuses people. **SSCP** is for the people who _operate_ controls — sysadmins, network admins, security admins — and needs only one year of experience. **CISSP** is for people who _design and govern_ them. If your daily work is configuring, SSCP (or vendor certs like [Cisco](/certifications/cisco-certifications) / [Microsoft](/certifications/microsoft-certifications) tracks) fits; if your work is writing policy, leading audits and owning risk decisions, CISSP is the target.

## CCSP: the cloud specialist that pays

Cloud security roles outgrow every other security specialty, and CCSP is its recognized certificate: cloud concepts, architecture, governance/risk/compliance in cloud, data security, platform security, operations, legal. Eligibility: five years in IT including one year of cloud security — and a current CISSP counts as one full year, making CCSP a natural _second_ certificate for CISSP holders who moved into cloud work (for a deeper cloud-architecture alternative, see [AWS Certifications](/certifications/aws-certifications) security tracks).

## CGRC and the GRC niche

If your direction is compliance, assessment and authorization work (the world of [Compliance Frameworks](/grc/compliance-frameworks)), CGRC certifies exactly that: security/risk in acquisition, control assessment, continuous monitoring, authorization decisions. It is niche but scarce — GRC roles frequently list "CISSP or CGRC" with few applicants holding either.

## Practical playbook

1. **Entry**: CC (free) _or_ Security+ — one is enough; both is redundant.
2. **Practitioner years**: vendor certificates matching your stack + SSCP once you pass the one-year mark.
3. **The jump**: CISSP when you have the experience banked and a realistic path to endorsement (a former manager who is ISC2-certified is gold — cultivate the reference early).
4. **Specialize**: CCSP for cloud, CGRC for GRC, CSSLP for AppSec — pick the one your last two job ads already mention.
5. **Budget the bureaucracy**: AMF per year + CPE logging from day one. Lapsed certificates must be re-earned by exam; the CPE spreadsheet is part of the job now.
